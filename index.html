I. Academic & Professional Overview

David Terrence Giberti is a Staff Design Verification Engineer and Team Lead at Qualcomm Incorporated in Austin, Texas, with over fourteen years of industrial hardware engineering experience spanning processor microarchitecture, pre-silicon design verification, C++ co-simulation infrastructure, and post-silicon microprocessor bring-up. In his current appointment within Qualcomm's Adreno GPU Shader Processor organization, he manages, guides, and mentors a team of verification engineers responsible for the pre-silicon verification of hardware-accelerated ray tracing and matrix compute subsystems—specifically the Ray Tracing Unit (RTU), Tree Traversal Unit (TTU), Ray Tracing Tree Traversal Unit (RTTU), and Matrix Arithmetic Logic Unit (MALU)—while architecting and refactoring C++ Direct Programming Interface (DPI-C) co-simulation frameworks that bridge register-transfer level (RTL) verification environments with repository-based C++ functional and performance models.

Prior to joining Qualcomm, Mr. Giberti held engineering appointments at Advanced Micro Devices (AMD), where he conducted x86-64 post-silicon validation, emulation debug, and C++/assembly diagnostic development for flagship EPYC server and Ryzen client processors; Cirrus Logic, where he verified mixed-signal and digital integrated circuits; the Samsung Austin Research Center (SARC), where he performed pre-silicon SystemVerilog/UVM verification on custom CPU core Load/Store Units (LSU) and Translation Lookaside Buffers (TLB); and Arm Ltd., where he verified CPU core frontend pipelines (Instruction Fetch, Decode, Register Rename, and ALU through Commit) and engineered automated Jenkins CI/CD synthesis and physical place-and-route timing analysis pipelines. His industrial practice is complemented by graduate research and coursework in computer architecture simulation (C++ SESC), out-of-order hardware design (SystemVerilog Tomasulo RTL), formal verification (Synopsys VC-Formal), distributed operating systems, and machine learning across the Georgia Institute of Technology, San José State University, the University of California, Santa Cruz, and The University of Texas at Austin.
